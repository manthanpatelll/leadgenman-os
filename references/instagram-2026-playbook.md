# Instagram 2026 Playbook

What actually moves reach on Instagram right now, based on platform statements and my own account data. Reference this before drafting any reel or carousel.

## How reels get ranked

Confirmed ranking order for Reels:

1. **Watch time.** Drives initial distribution.
2. **Sends per reach.** DM shares, weighted 3 to 5 times more than likes for non-follower reach.
3. **Likes per reach.**

Practical translation: I optimise for sends, not likes. A reel someone forwards to a friend beats a reel someone double-taps.

## Platform signals I build around

- Original content gets 40 to 60% more distribution than reposts.
- Accounts posting 10+ reposts in 30 days are excluded from recommendations entirely.
- TikTok watermarks kill reach. AI-generated slop is a penalty signal.
- "Raw, real human content" is the stated priority. Talking head plus real screen recordings beats fully rendered visuals.
- Users can now see and edit the topic categories Instagram uses for them. Getting categorised correctly (AI coding, Claude Code, automation) matters more than ever.
- Instagram is pushing Reels harder in 2026, not pivoting back to photos.
- Trial Reels give roughly +80% reach among non-followers. Free test bed for hooks before a full build.
- Reels made in the native Edits app get a small algorithm bump.

## Hook rules

- **The hook is literally everything.** If the hook is weak, the value never gets seen. Bad hook means kill the concept, don't try to save it with better content downstream.
- **Two lines, always.** The on-screen hook wraps across two balanced lines. A single line runs edge to edge in the safe zone and reads cramped on a phone. Even a two-word hook goes on two lines.
- **Name two ingredients.** Claude Code plus a specific second tool by name. "Figma", "Stitch", "Xcode", not "a design tool".
- **Open on the new thing.** No callbacks to a previous reel. Cold viewers never saw it.
- Hook archetypes that win: "Instead of [time-waste], do [tool play]", "[Tool A] vs [Tool B]", "Stop paying [$N], use [free alternative]", "I've used [tool] for [time]. Here's [setup].", cinematic surprise in 7 seconds with no setup.
- Hook archetypes that die: "Here are N tools", "This is the future of", "I automated my entire", preachy "never be this person", conditional "if you're [this] and not doing [that]".

## Safe zones (1080 x 1920)

| Zone | Pixels |
|---|---|
| Vertical safe | y = 280 to y = 1520 |
| Horizontal safe | x = 60 to x = 1020 |
| Titles | Never above y = 300 |
| Bottom content | Never below y = 1500 |

Instagram overlays username, follow button and status bar at the top, caption and action buttons at the bottom. Anything placed there is unreadable. Applies to every card edge, not just text.

## Retention rules for animated reels

- Everything visible on frame zero. No fade-in, no pop-in, no spring entry. A blank first frame is an instant scroll-away.
- No fade-out, no loop. Hold the final frame at full opacity.
- The payoff (the final reveal) holds for at least 25 to 30% of the total duration. Never cut the meat of the sandwich. If that makes the rest feel rushed, extend the clip.
- Keep something alive during the payoff hold: a pulsing dot, a subtle breathing motion. Never a frozen frame.
- No captions burned into the animation. Text is added in the edit.

## Carousel behaviour

- **Distribution model.** Carousels go to followers first, prove engagement, then hit the explore page. Reels reach roughly 50% non-followers from day one. Carousels are the retention and saves tool, reels are the acquisition tool. I need both.
- **Slide count.** 8 to 12 slides is the sweet spot for saves. 17 slides flopped.
- **Aspect.** 1080 x 1350 (4:5) or 1080 x 1440 (3:4) per slide.
- **Cover design.** The cover is the hook. My best performers use real interface chrome (macOS Finder, VS Code) with a cream background so the cover reads as a screenshot of something real, not a graphic. Body text stays 50px inside the edge, only display text crosses slide boundaries.
- **The swipe.** Slide one earns the tap, slide two earns the swipe. Seamless designs where an element visibly continues onto the next slide pull the swipe on their own, but the crossing element must be bold and high opacity or it doesn't register.
- **CTA slide.** Keyword in curly double quotes, "follow for more.", @leadgenman.
- **Captions.** 500+ word captions averaged 6,866 likes versus 2,759 for medium length. Long captions win.
- **Hashtags.** None. Posts without hashtags averaged 3,381 likes versus 1,479 with them.
- **Export.** 4x minimum, 8x for gold standard designs.

## Power word categories

Two or three per hook, maximum. Each one should be a knife. Drop generic words and keep only the scroll-stoppers.

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Curiosity | secret, hidden, nobody knows, before |
| Authority | official, named experts, top 1% |
| Scarcity / urgency | just, first, right now, today |
| Contrarian | stop, you're wrong, obsolete, garbage |
| Outcome scale | 10K, $1M, 24/7, in minutes |
| Numbered | 3 things, 5 skills, 10 codes |
| Disruption | killed, replaces, destroys, ends |

## My niche on Instagram

AI coding on Instagram is underbuilt compared to YouTube. Public benchmark data for the niche is thin, so my own historical reels are a better guide than any industry report. At my size I'm one of the larger dedicated AI coding accounts on the platform, which means I'm building in a low-data lane and my own numbers are the benchmark.
